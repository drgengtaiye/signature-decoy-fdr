Validation Cohort Lookup

A prognostic gene model fitted in TCGA is often called validated when its high-risk group fares worse in a public cohort. How often does a model built from genes no published theme or model uses pass that same test there? Each bar is the share of null models that did: the usual pipeline (univariate Cox screen, then LASSO Cox) run in TCGA of the cohort's cancer type on pools of genes matched in number (six of every eight also in co-expression) to 15 published themes, such as ferroptosis and hypoxia, but drawn from genes outside all of them, then tested with one rule (median split, log-rank P < 0.05, high group worse). The rates run from 0% to 85%.

Reading a row

The bar is the share of null models that validated in the cohort, with the count beside it. A model of genes chosen for no reason passes there that often, so a validation in a cohort with a long bar says less than one in a cohort with a short bar. Within a cancer type, cohorts differ beyond chance, and across the 35 cohorts that share no patients the rate was only weakly related to the number of events (Spearman 0.24, one-sided P = 0.08).

The right column gives the validation rate of published models of the cancer type, among papers that did not name the cohort as a validation cohort and among those that did. A cohort marked same patients shares its patients with another cohort and carries that cohort's rates; one marked overlaps shares some patients with another cohort of the table and has its own rate.
